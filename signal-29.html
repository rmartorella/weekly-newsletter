One

What we are seeing

The fastest-growing AI purchase is not building AI.

The AI briefs everyone expects are there. Agent builds, enablement programmes, governance policy, architecture validation. But sitting alongside them is a category nobody has a name for yet and it is growing faster than the ones that do. A software company commissioned mystery shopping and live demos of its rivals AI features, explicitly to learn what the product does in a demo versus what the marketing claims. An adjacent brief benchmarked the price of the human alternative so an AI feature could be sold on savings. The motive, in one client own framing, was to counter a legacy narrative being used against them in deals they were losing.

That is a defensive positioning purchase wearing the clothes of market research, and it needs primary research and demo-evaluation skill rather than engineering. Which means the people best placed to win it are not the people currently pitching for AI work. If your background is interviews and synthesis rather than architecture, this category is wide open and almost nobody is positioned for it.

Demand trends

Two sectors took share this year and three gave it up. Because these are shares, a gain implies somebody else losing ground, which is a more useful frame than raw growth. Alongside that we can now measure how long a won project actually takes to sign, and the difference between a sponsor buyer and a corporate one is larger than anything else we have looked at.

Buyer decision trends

When clients search for a consultant they do not browse, they type. And the strings are more sophisticated than most people assume, combining a firm pedigree, a piece of situation language, and an industry in a single query. The vocabulary is also shifting. Value creation language is displacing pure strategy language, and operator words are displacing advisory ones.

Supply trends

The useful version of supply and demand is that the pools people crowd into are not the pools where the work is. The thinnest capability pools on the platform are also some of the steadiest sources of demand, and the largest pools are the most contested. Repositioning into an adjacent, thinner area is usually a bigger lever than getting better at the crowded one.

Outcomes

We started collecting structured outcome reports in June and the volume is climbing fast. The interesting finding is not the results themselves, it is how consultants describe why they were right for the work after it went well. They lead with prior sector exposure and a named functional depth. Almost nobody leads with credentials, which lines up exactly with what buyers tell us they want.

So whatRewrite one of your case studies in this shape: what was broken, what you installed, and who owns it now. Results that describe something handed over land far better than results that describe something recommended.

How to read the numbers. Everything about client demand is shown as a share, a direction or a rank, never as a count, a client name or a deal value. Our clients tell us things on the condition that we do not repeat them. Supply-side figures such as rates and practice area pool sizes are our own network’s data and are shown directly.

Where it comes from. The Signal is written from the project briefs submitted to Catalant and the client conversations our team records, read in full each week. Nothing in it identifies a client, a company or a transaction. AI mention rates are measured on full call transcripts rather than call summaries, which compress and undercount by roughly half. Sales cycle is measured from the brief landing to a contract being created, on closed-won projects only. A private equity brief means the buying account sits in one of our PE client communities or the brief itself names a sponsor; internal and test records are excluded from every denominator.
