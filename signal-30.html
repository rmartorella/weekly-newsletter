One

What we are seeing

The handoff is the deliverable now.

A quiet inversion is showing up across healthcare and consumer briefs. The classic interim engagement was defined by a gap: someone left, something needs doing, cover it. What is arriving now describes the exit before it describes the work. One brief required the consultant to document the processes and stay on to train the eventual permanent hire. Another described the goal as a smooth handover rather than a disruptive back to back transition. The analysis is table stakes. The handover is the product.

Underneath it is a fact worth sitting with. The trigger for most of these is not a project, it is a failed or delayed permanent search, and in several cases the interim exists precisely because the headcount has not been approved yet. Which means your competition on these briefs is not another consultant, it is a recruiter, and the way you get extended is by being visibly good at making yourself unnecessary. That inverts the usual play, because indispensability reads here as a failure to deliver the actual deliverable.

Two

The data

Demand trends

Two things are happening to the shape of the work at once and they point the same way. Engagements are getting longer, and the share asking for some physical presence has been climbing for a year after a long flat stretch. Short, remote, scoped analytical pieces are the slice getting squeezed. Sitting behind both is the sheer weight of the private equity buyer, which puts deal clocks on work that used to run on annual planning cycles.

Read it as: briefs of under a month have gone from roughly a quarter of the market to about one in seven. Five months and longer is now close to a third. And on-site is creeping back up, which almost nobody would have predicted eighteen months ago.
So whatWiden your stated availability. If your profile says you take work under a month you are opting out of about a third of the market, and the fastest-shrinking third is the one you are opting into. Also check whether your travel position is stricter than it needs to be.

Who is buying: the private equity share keeps climbingMinimum monthly

% of briefs, by quarter
Read it as: around six in ten briefs now come from a private equity firm or a PE-backed company, and integration has doubled alongside it. The two move together because they are the same phenomenon. When the carve-out line rises this steadily it usually means a wave of separation work is about to land.
So whatIf you have never worked a private equity deal, get one reference engagement on the board this year even at a lower number. These buyers reuse people, and around six in ten of the briefs we see now come through them. Treating private equity as a niche is the most common positioning mistake we see.

Buyer decision trends

We read every engagement that stalled out this year and the result surprised us. Almost none were lost to a competitor. They stalled on fiscal timing, an approval process, or a missing internal champion. Which means a no in the third quarter is usually a not yet, and the highest-return follow-up available to you is showing back up in the client budget-build month with something already scoped and priced.

So whatBuild a follow-up list by fiscal year, not by how the call felt. Find the budget-build month for your top five accounts and put a reminder in for it with something already scoped and priced. That single habit converts more than another round of chasing.

Supply trends

The single cheapest change most people could make is to their first two lines. When clients search for consultants they type boolean strings: a tier-one firm name, a piece of value creation language, and an industry, all in one query. If those literal words are not in your profile you do not appear, no matter how well you would do the work.

This month, change one thing

Rewrite your first line so it names the line job, not the method. Not "transformation leader with expertise in operating model design" but "ran supply chain for a $400M industrial through a carve-out." The first is a category anyone can claim. The second is a fact only you can. Then add one redacted page of real work product, because almost nobody does and the ones who do are visibly separating themselves.

Outcomes

How work is priced is quietly becoming a positioning decision rather than an administrative one. This year fixed fee overtook hourly as the single most common structure on the platform, and the shift away from time and materials has been steady rather than sudden. That matters to you because a fixed fee is a bet on your own scoping, and the people making that bet are being paid for outcomes rather than for hours.

So whatQuote a fixed fee on your next well-scoped piece of work. Start with something you have done before so the scoping risk is low. The market is moving toward the people who will price the outcome, and the first one is the hardest.

How to read the numbers. Everything about client demand is shown as a share, a direction or a rank, never as a count, a client name or a deal value. Our clients tell us things on the condition that we do not repeat them. Supply-side figures such as rates and practice area pool sizes are our own network’s data and are shown directly.

Where it comes from. The Signal is written from the project briefs submitted to Catalant and the client conversations our team records, read in full each week. Nothing in it identifies a client, a company or a transaction. AI mention rates are measured on full call transcripts rather than call summaries, which compress and undercount by roughly half. Sales cycle is measured from the brief landing to a contract being created, on closed-won projects only. A private equity brief means the buying account sits in one of our PE client communities or the brief itself names a sponsor; internal and test records are excluded from every denominator.
