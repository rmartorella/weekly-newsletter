One

What we are seeing

Quality and reliability, without the rest of it.

About half the briefs we get now include a sentence that has nothing to do with the work. Not a traditional big-firm advisory approach. The client does not seek recommendations or theories. This is not a role for a passive documenter. We are not seeking a team lead or a partner, low ego. For a while I read those as clients turning against the big firms, and I no longer think that is right. Every one of those briefs still asks for the pedigree, and they still want the things a good firm genuinely delivers: rigour, a real answer, somebody who has done this before, and the confidence that it actually gets finished.

What they are refusing is everything that has historically come attached to it. The layers, the bill, the theatre, and the person who needs the room to know how senior they are. That is not a rejection of consulting, it is a far more specific order than we used to get, and it is a hard order for a firm to fill and a natural one for an experienced operator working on their own. The consultants doing well here are not winning because they are cheaper. They are winning because they can be trusted to deliver like a firm and behave like a colleague, and the data further down this issue says the same thing from three different directions.

The data

Demand trends

The most useful number we have on AI is not how much of it we are selling. It is the distance between how often clients raise it in the room and how often it survives into a brief. That gap has widened every year for three years running, and a gap that keeps widening is a forecast rather than a curiosity. Underneath it the wider mix is moving the same way, toward work that gets installed and away from work that gets presented.

Said versus bought: AI in the conversation against AI in the briefMinimum monthly

Read it as: AI now comes up in 44% of client conversations, close to triple where it was two years ago. It survives into 19% of the briefs those conversations produce. The gap has gone from ten points to twenty five, which means the talking is running well ahead of the buying and most of it has not been scoped yet. The people who get scoped into it will be the ones already in the room when it stops being a conversation. Method: full call transcripts, not summaries. Summaries compress and undercount this by roughly half.
So whatGet into the conversation before it becomes a brief. Ask your current clients what they are trying to do with AI, even where it is nowhere near your scope, and put one line in your profile naming the AI-adjacent work you have actually shipped. Twenty five points of demand is sitting unscoped, and it will be scoped by whoever is already in the room.

Buyer decision trends

The most repeated rejection in our debriefs is not about capability. It is a version of I could not tell what I would actually get. Very few of the conversations we sit in on include a consultant putting an actual piece of work on the table, which makes one redacted deliverable from a comparable engagement rarer and more useful than a strong CV. The second most common loss reason is one nobody ever hears about, which is being too senior for the job the client actually needs done.

So whatTake one redacted page of real work product into your next first call. Not a CV, not a capability deck, an actual deliverable from a comparable engagement. Almost nobody does this, and it answers the exact question that is losing people the work.

Supply trends

This is the question we are asked more than any other, so here is the answer. Density varies more by type of work than by anything else. Diligence briefs draw a crowd because the skill reads as portable. Interim operating seats draw far fewer people, because the commitment is longer and the fit is narrower, and that is exactly why the odds there are better.

What the stages mean. Our matching system pulls a long list of consultants against every brief. A Catalant expert lead then screens that list and approves or declines each person, which is the screening step. The ones approved are invited to express interest, and a short slate goes to the client by name. Read it as: the narrow point is not the client. It is the step from matched to screened, which is decided on how legible your profile is against the brief. That is also the step most within your control, and the supply section of issue 28 shows exactly what separates the people who clear it.
So whatStop optimising for the client and start optimising for the screen. The step that removes most people is a Catalant lead reading your profile against a brief, so make sure your profile contains the words the brief will contain. That is a profile edit, not a sales effort.

Outcomes

Rates are what people have least visibility into and most anxiety about, so we will publish the shape of them regularly. Two notes on method. We will always talk in weekly and daily terms and never hourly, because hourly framing drags everyone toward the same commodity conversation. And the honest headline is that rates barely separate by practice area at all, which tells you rate is almost never the thing deciding an engagement.

This is why every median we publish is a round number. Five prices account for 76% of the entire market. $6,000, $8,000, $10,000, $12,000 and $14,000. Across 17,210 consultants there are only 272 distinct rates on the platform and 91.6% are an exact multiple of $1,000. The median lands on $8,000 because that is where the market physically sits, not because we rounded it. For context: practice area barely moves this. Every one of the eight largest practice areas has a median between $10,000 and $12,000 on the curated bench, and the spread inside each is wider than the gap between them.
So whatNotice how empty the odd numbers are. Fewer than one in fifty consultants sits at $9,000 or $11,000, which means the gaps between the big five are nearly unoccupied. If you are at $8,000 and delivering like the people at $12,000, there is no crowd standing between you and a move, and no client is comparing you against a dense field at $9,000. Pick your number off this chart deliberately rather than inheriting the one you set when you started.

How to read the numbers. Everything about client demand is shown as a share, a direction or a rank, never as a count, a client name or a deal value. Our clients tell us things on the condition that we do not repeat them. Supply-side figures such as rates and practice area pool sizes are our own network’s data and are shown directly.

Where it comes from. The Signal is written from the project briefs submitted to Catalant and the client conversations our team records, read in full each week. Nothing in it identifies a client, a company or a transaction. AI mention rates are measured on full call transcripts rather than call summaries, which compress and undercount by roughly half. Sales cycle is measured from the brief landing to a contract being created, on closed-won projects only. A private equity brief means the buying account sits in one of our PE client communities or the brief itself names a sponsor; internal and test records are excluded from every denominator.
